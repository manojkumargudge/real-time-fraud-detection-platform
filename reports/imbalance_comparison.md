# Class-imbalance comparison

Baseline XGBoost, same features and chronological split; methods `scale_pos_weight` (current: negatives / positives of the training rows) and `none` (weight 1); seeds 42, 7, 2026; trained one at a time; every run logged to MLflow experiment `fraud-xgboost`. Generated by `python -m src.models.imbalance_comparison`.

Operating points: *threshold* = each run's own max-F1 threshold from the training holdout (the serving alert rule); *1% budget* = alert on the top 1% of validation scores (Milestone 2.2 budget).

| Method | Weight | Runs | Mean PR-AUC | Min | Max | Precision @ threshold | Recall @ threshold | Alert rate @ threshold | Precision @ 1% budget | Recall @ 1% budget | ROC-AUC (ref.) | Mean train s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `scale_pos_weight` | 27.46 | 3 | 0.2517 | 0.2457 | 0.2591 | 0.2682 | 0.4087 | 5.25% | 0.4532 | 0.1318 | 0.8371 | 45 |
| `none` | 1.00 | 3 | 0.2935 | 0.2877 | 0.2992 | 0.3098 | 0.3849 | 4.27% | 0.5282 | 0.1536 | 0.8459 | 70 |

`none` has the higher mean validation PR-AUC (0.2935 for `none` vs 0.2517 for `scale_pos_weight`, difference +0.0418). The difference is larger than the seed-to-seed spread (0.0134, the larger max-minus-min PR-AUC across seeds of the two methods). ROC-AUC is shown for reference but not used for the decision, because with about 3.5% fraud it is dominated by the many easy negatives and hides differences in the high-score region that alerting depends on.

Champion: moved to `none` seed 42 (registered as `fraud-xgboost` version 2); the served bundle in `artifacts/baseline/` is unchanged until deployed.
